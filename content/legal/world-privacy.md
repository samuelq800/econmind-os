## 03. Formal Commands, Events & Receipts

### 3.1 Scope of the Economic Record

The system records formally processed economic operations through a **command–event–receipt** structure. A command identifies the submitted economic instruction; related events record resulting economic facts where applicable; the receipt records its processing outcome and associated links.

Relevant information includes the account or authentication identifier and responsible actor; the world, economy, and Office where applicable; command type and parameters; real and simulation timestamps; command and correlation references; duplicate-prevention identifiers; relevant World State versions; and the result, related events, and reason for rejection where applicable. Authorisation versions and membership information support the relevant access and authority checks.

The clock uses **10× progression**: **one second of effective real time corresponds to ten seconds of simulation time**, and the simulated year contains **360 simulated days**. Applicable pause and resume rules determine effective running time. The real and simulated timestamps therefore serve different purposes and are not interchangeable.

### 3.2 Processing Outcomes & Corrections

The command record distinguishes successful processing, rejection within the command system, and an inability to proceed following a change in authorisation. A request rejected before it enters the command system is not necessarily represented by a persistent command record. A rejected command need not produce a change in economic state.

Authoritative economic history is append-only: established facts are not silently overwritten, and corrections are expressed through linked subsequent events. Revoking an authorisation, removing a participant, or withdrawing consent for a separate optional use does not, by itself, reverse a completed economic transaction or erase an accepted simulation contract. The personal-information implications of continued retention remain subject to Section 08 and applicable law.

### 3.3 Gameplay Records & Browsing Behaviour

Formal command recording is not a claim that all browsing, scrolling, typing, or interface clicks are captured as economic decisions. A preview, local draft, or unsubmitted intention is not, merely by appearing in the interface, a settled command.

Account sessions and essential browser storage support authentication, preferences, and drafts under the general platform arrangements. Hosting and service infrastructure may also process connection, operational, or security information for service delivery and protection. Such processing is distinct from the official economic command record and remains limited by its applicable purpose and notice. It must not be inferred that no technical information is handled merely because an interaction is not a gameplay command.

## 04. Purposes & Limits of Use

Personal information is used as necessary to authenticate participants; maintain membership, economy, and Office assignments; verify authority and information access; process and settle formal economic operations; preserve the meaning and integrity of relevant records; administer competitions; address errors, support requests, and misuse; and provide reports to their authorised audiences.

Processing must have a specified purpose, be limited to the information necessary for that purpose, and be supported by an applicable lawful basis. Acceptance of Terms or participation in a school team is not consent to every subsequent use. Where consent or a separate authorisation is required, it must be obtained for the relevant purpose; withdrawal operates subject to applicable law and does not retrospectively invalidate processing already lawfully undertaken.

EconMind does not sell personal information or use it to build behavioural advertising profiles. A general educational purpose does not authorise unrestricted publicity, unrelated research, or undisclosed individual profiling. Any materially different purpose, audience, or processing arrangement must be addressed through appropriate notice and any required authorisation.

## 05. Information Access & Visibility

Access is determined through server-verified identity, valid membership, economy, Office assignment where applicable, and current authorisation. Hiding a button, page, or menu is not the access-control boundary. Being a participant, contributor, administrator, or member of the technical department does not confer unrestricted access to all information.

| Scope | Access boundary |
| --- | --- |
| **Public** | Information designated for lawful public release, including the corresponding public world, country, or results material. Publication of selected source data does not make all live or private records public. |
| **National** | Information authorised for members of the relevant economy. Membership of one economy does not confer access to another economy's private records. |
| **Office-private** | Information restricted to the relevant economy and authorised Office assignment. National membership alone does not grant every Office-private permission. |
| **Negotiation-party** | Information restricted to the authorised parties to the relevant negotiation or arrangement. It is not generally available merely because other users participate in the same world. |
| **Administrative** | Information available within the authorised administrative scope for the relevant operational or competition responsibility. Administrative access does not confer unrestricted authority to rewrite the economic ledger. |

Technical and competition personnel may handle records only within their actual assigned authority and permitted purpose. Their department or job title is not a substitute for an access decision. Email addresses, authentication identifiers, private drafts, support correspondence, and administrative notes are not public-directory material merely because they concern a participant.

Public world and country source previews identify the nature of the material they display. A selected source dataset is not, merely by being officially published, a live authoritative World State or permission to access restricted participant records.

## 06. Final Simulation Reports & Public Results

The platform generates Final Simulation Reports from formal economic operations and related simulation data. Their purposes include decision review, economic performance analysis, policy timelines, strategic evaluation, team reflection, and academic discussion. A Final Simulation Report is distinct from a final settlement or processing receipt for an individual command.

The **Full Report** is accessible to the relevant team and authorised competition personnel, subject to applicable access and confidentiality requirements. That audience does not imply inclusion of every Office-private record, third-party negotiation detail, or unrelated personal record. Report content must respect the access and disclosure boundaries applicable to its sources.

The **Public Report** is a controlled version from which appropriate personal, restricted, or confidential information is excluded. EconMind manages its publication under the competition rules and applicable privacy requirements. Identifying details that are unnecessary for the authorised publication are excluded; any permitted identifiable disclosure requires its own lawful basis and any required authorisation.

Reports distinguish recorded actions and outcomes from participant-supplied explanations and analytical interpretation. An action log does not, by itself, establish private reasoning, motive, or political belief. Simulated results and correlations are not empirical proof of real-world causation or certification of a participant's professional competence.

Internal report access does not automatically authorise institutional promotion, publication of a Full Report, or research involving identifiable information. Team representatives cannot consent on behalf of other persons solely by holding a Captain or other Office role. Any additional disclosure or research use must remain within its lawful, notified purpose and permitted scope.

## 08. Retention, Identity Links & Deletion

Personal information must be retained only for as long as necessary for the disclosed purpose and any applicable legal obligation. Account and role information, command and event history, support and integrity records, reports, identity links, technical logs, and backups may require different treatment. Relevant considerations include the operation of the account or season, the preservation of legitimate shared records, resolution of a dispute or integrity issue, security needs, and applicable legal duties.

Different categories may have different lawful grounds and criteria for retention. Their purposes and the assessment of continued necessity are distinguished as follows:

| Data category | Retention purpose and assessment criteria |
| --- | --- |
| **A. Account & Identity Data** | Supports account identity, authorisation, membership, and necessary account administration. Continued retention is assessed against the account's status, valid access and membership arrangements, necessary administration, and applicable legal duties. |
| **B. Simulation Commands & Event Records** | Preserves economic-state integrity, authoritative event history, transaction verification, and simulation consistency. The need to preserve economic facts is assessed separately from the necessity of retaining personal identifiers or identity links. |
| **C. Competition Administration Records** | Supports competition administration, confirmation of results, necessary review, and related dispute handling. Continued retention is assessed against the relevant activity and any unresolved administrative, integrity, or dispute matter, together with applicable legal duties. |
| **D. Final Simulation Reports** | Supports post-season analysis, team reflection, and presentation of achievements within the applicable rules. Continued retention is assessed against those purposes, the authorised report audience, and the necessity of retaining any identifying information. |
| **E. Technical & Security Logs** | Supports system security, error investigation, access control, and necessary technical maintenance. Continued retention is assessed against the relevant security, diagnostic, or maintenance need and applicable legal duties. |
| **F. Backups & Recovery Data** | Supports applicable system-recovery and data-integrity requirements. Continued retention and disposal are assessed against those requirements, applicable law, and the actual recovery and deletion capabilities of the relevant systems. |

An append-only ledger protects the integrity of economic facts; it is not a policy of permanent identifiable retention. Correction of an economic event is also distinct from correction or deletion of a person's profile information. The need to preserve a simulation history does not remove personal-information duties.

Leaving an Office, leaving a team, withdrawing from a competition, and deleting an account are different actions. Loss of access does not automatically remove every shared historical record. Conversely, a connection to World does not create a blanket exemption from lawful deletion, restriction, or removal of unnecessary identity links.

Account and privacy requests are considered under the applicable platform arrangements and law, taking account of other participants' legitimate records. This Notice does not guarantee automated deletion of World-linked accounts, erasure of authoritative transactions, immediate removal from all backups, or a particular fixed retention period.

Where information is de-identified, its remaining context must be considered. A replacement identifier or removal of a name does not necessarily make a record anonymous: school, Office, timing, and activity may still allow identification. Information must not be treated as outside personal-information protections merely because it has been labelled de-identified.

Retention and deletion must be handled in accordance with applicable law and the actual technical capabilities of the relevant systems. A technical limitation does not, by itself, remove applicable legal obligations.
